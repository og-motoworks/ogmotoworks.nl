Motorcycle maintenance: what and when

What is in a minor and a major service, how often does your motorcycle need servicing and which signs should you not ignore? We explain it in this guide.

Why servicing your motorcycle matters

Motorcycles in the Netherlands have no APK (MOT). So nobody is required to look at your brakes, tyres or lights. On two wheels you also notice wear sooner: a worn tyre, spongy brakes or a dry chain directly affect your safety. With regular servicing your bike stays reliable, you avoid expensive repairs and it holds its value better.

Minor and major service: the difference

A minor service covers the basics: changing the engine oil and oil filter and a safety check of brakes, tyres, chain, lights and controls. A major service adds the parts your manufacturer's schedule only calls for after a certain number of kilometres or years, such as spark plugs, air filter, brake fluid, coolant and checking the valves.

Which service is due differs per make and model. The mileage and the date of the last service decide it together.

How often does your motorcycle need servicing?

The answer is in the service schedule in your owner's manual. It gives an interval in kilometres and in time, whichever comes first. If you ride few kilometres, a service once a year is usually still wise: oil, brake fluid and tyres also age when your bike stands still.

Lost the manual? Send us your make, model, year and mileage and we'll find out which service is due.

Checklist: what you check yourself in between

  • Tyre pressure: check it regularly, with cold tyres.
  • Tread and damage: look for cracks, bulges and embedded nails or stones.
  • Chain: is it lubricated and does it have the right slack according to the manual?
  • Brakes: does the lever feel firm and is there enough pad left?
  • Oil level: check the level as described in the manual.
  • Lights: do the headlight, tail light, brake light and indicators work?

Signs your motorcycle needs attention

  • A fault or warning light is on. Read warning light.
  • Your bike starts badly, hesitates or runs rough. Read hesitation.
  • You see oil under your bike or on the forks. Read oil leak.
  • The brakes squeal, grind or feel spongy. Read brakes.
  • The chain rattles or keeps needing adjustment. Read chain noise.

Do it yourself or have it done?

Lubricating the chain and checking tyre pressure and oil level are fine to do yourself. Work on brakes, valves, forks or electronics needs experience and the right tools. A mistake there costs safety or an expensive repair. Not sure? Message us and we'll think along about what you can do yourself and what is better left to us.

Before and after winter

If you lay your bike up for winter, an autumn service is a good moment: with fresh oil and a well-charged battery it comes through winter better. See our winter packages. In spring, check tyre pressure, brakes and chain before your first ride.
