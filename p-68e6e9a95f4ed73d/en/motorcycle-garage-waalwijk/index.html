What kind of help do you need?

Not everyone knows in advance whether their bike needs a service, a repair or a diagnosis first. That's fine. Here is a short guide so you roughly know where to start. Not sure? Just message us what you notice and we'll think along.

  • Your bike runs fine, but a service is due: choose motorcycle servicing. We follow your manufacturer's schedule and check the parts that wear.
  • Something leaks, you hear a strange noise or something feels different: that is a repair. We first find the cause and discuss the options with you.
  • A warning light is on or it hesitates for no clear reason: start with diagnostics and fault finding. See also warning light.
  • Your tyres are worn or you're unsure which to choose: see motorcycle tyres. We recommend a tyre that suits your bike and your riding style.

What can you expect from us?

At OG MotoWorks you deal with the person who actually works on your bike. You message Freddy, and Freddy does the work. So no information gets lost along the way and you get answers from someone who has seen your bike. We explain in plain language what is going on, and if you like we show you the worn parts. Which makes and models do we work on?

We work on motorcycles of all makes, from Japanese naked bikes and sports bikes to European touring bikes and adventure bikes. Older bikes with carburettors are welcome too: that is often where the work is that newer bikes no longer need, such as cleaning, adjusting and synchronising carburettors. On modern bikes with a lot of electronics we read out the bike to find faults. We only work on motorcycles, not on scooters or mopeds.

What does a job with us look like?

Honda CBR600F: bucking and stuttering once warm

A 2000 Honda CBR600F only ran properly with the choke on. As soon as it warmed up, it bucked, stuttered and lost power. The cause was in the float bowls. We opened, cleaned and overhauled the carburettors, then adjusted and synchronised them. While we were at it, we also lubricated the chain and checked the tyre pressure. The result: a CBR that pulls the way it should again.

BMW M 1000 R: a clutch that partly engaged by itself

The rider of a BMW M 1000 R noticed the clutch was slightly operating by itself. In our workshop we found the cause: too little free play on the clutch. After adjusting the free play the clutch gripped fully again.

Both jobs show how we work: first find out what is really wrong, then replace or adjust parts. See more examples on recent work.
